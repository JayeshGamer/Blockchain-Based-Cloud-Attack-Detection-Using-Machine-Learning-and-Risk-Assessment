ABSTRACT
The distributed denial of service (DDoS) attacks represent a real danger on cloud computing systems, the existing security
approaches have notable limitations such as centralization, lack of adaptability and scalability, and focusing on detection over
mitigation.Inthispaper,wepresentanovelblockchain-basedsystemtoenhanceattacktoleranceofcloudsystemsagainstDDoS
attacksbyintegratingahierarchicalrisk-basedcloudattacktolerancemechanismwithsmartcontracts,Theproposedapproach
ensurescomprehensivethreatdetectionandmitigationbyestimatingrisksatallthreelayers:local,cluster,andglobaldynamically
using Dempster–Shafer theory. Simulations validated the superiority of our approach compared to similar systems in terms of
availability,attackdetectionrate,andpacketlossmetricsinlarge-scalecloudsimulationof10,000nodes.Theresultsdemonstrate
thatoursystemnotonlyimprovessecuritybutalsomaintainsservicequalityunderhigh-intensityattackscenarios.Additionally,
theimplementationofourblockchainarchitectureshowssignificantscalabilityandcost-efficiency,thus,confirmingitssuitability
for large cloud deployments. Furthermore, the reliability of the smart contracts against potential threats were confirmed by a
formalsecurityanalysisusingtheEchidnatool.

TheproposedsystemoffersdetectionandmitigationthroughSCs

This comprehensive approach not only detects threats but also
dynamically adjusts security protocols to mitigate these threats

records all data on the blockchain. Our enhanced blockchain
ensures transparency and decentralization, while our SCs auto-
mate risk assessment, threshold adjustments, and consensus
nodeselection.
The key contribution of this paper lies in the development of
a novel blockchain framework that merges SCs with a multi-
layered, hierarchical risk-based attack tolerance mechanism.
Unlike existing hybrid blockchain-IDS systems, our approach
introduces several distinct innovations. First, we perform risk
evaluation using DST at three levels: node, cluster, and global
enabling granular, scalable, and parallel threat assessment that
adaptsinreal-time.Second,thesystememploysdynamicthresh-
oldregulationandadaptiveweightadjustmentsbasedonevolving
risklevels,improvingaccuracy,andresponsivenessundervarying
attack conditions. Third, and most significantly, we propose a
novelrisk-awareconsensusmechanism.Byfocusingondynamic

components collectively contribute to a resilient, decentralized,
and adaptive defence framework capable of handling a broad
spectrumofattacktypes,goingbeyondtypicalDDoSmitigation
toincludeSybilattacks,insiderthreats,datapoisoning,andAPTs.
Our methodology addresses scalability by evaluating system
performance across a growing cloud environment from 1000 to
10,000 nodes using tailored metrics to assess attack tolerance,
communication overhead, and resource efficiency. Performance
evaluationisconductedinalargecloud,consideringfalsepositive
rate, false negative rate, detection rate, availability, and packet
lossrate.Gasconsumption,transactioncost,andexecutioncost
are also considered for practicality and efficiency. The security
analysisofthesmartcontractoftheproposedmodelisperformed
and evaluated using the Echidna security tool, focusing on key
functionalitiesandresilience.

2 RelatedWorks
Various attack tolerance mechanisms have been proposed in
the literature to address security issues in cloud environments,
particularly those targeting servers. This review covers current
and cloud-relevant insights from recent research and varied
techniques. This literature review section is organized into five
subsections: begin with IDS-based approaches, employing both
signature-basedandanomaly-basedintrusiondetectionsystems.
FollowwithAI-basedapproaches,emphasizinghowdeeplearn-
ing and other advanced AI techniques have been developed to
overcome some of the limitations of traditional IDS, such as
detectingunknownattacksandimprovingaccuracy.Next,discuss
isolation-based approaches that provide solutions to the limita-
tions of detection by preventing the spread of attacks once they
aredetected,showcasinghowresourceandnetworkisolationcan
mitigatetheimpactofattacksthatbypassdetectionmechanisms.
Then,exploreformalmethodsthatusemathematicalmodelling.

Hezavehi et al. [11] introduced an interactive anomaly-based
DDoS attack detection method in cloud computing, integrating

The method uses a threshold anomaly-based detection strategy

reducesfilteringstages.However,thismethodfaceschallengesin
detectinglow-rateDDoSattacks,lacksadaptabilitytonewattack
types, and sets fixed resource limits without proper evaluation.
Thestudyalsolackscomprehensivetestingagainstvariousattack

David et al. [12] proposed a dynamic threshold and entropy
measurementstatisticalmethodforeffectivelydetectingflooding
DDoS attacks in network traffic. They applied this method to
a virtual network using both DARPA and CAIDA datasets, and

However, the study has some limitations. It does not detect
application-layerDoSattacks,lackstechniquesforidentifyingor
mitigatingtheattackflows,anditsadaptabilityandscalabilityare
stillundefined.
Tsobdjou et al. [13] proposed a method to detect flooded DDoS

The study targets (Internet control message protocol) ICMP

evaluation. The system was tested through the GNS3 simula-

promising results in both real and simulated attack scenarios.
However, this approach faced challenges detecting low-DDoS
attacks;moreover,itneedsspecialfine-tuningofsomeparameters

Kautish et al. [14] introduced a scattered denial-of-service mit-
igation tree architecture, which aims to detect and mitigate
DDoS attacks in hybrid cloud environments. This framework
consists of three phases: filtering, attack detection, and attack
mitigation. The proposed model was tested on a hybrid-cloud
environment that combines Amazon Web Service EC2 with a
simulatedprivatecloud,usingtheKDD-99datasetforevaluation.
While the system shows high efficiency compared to current
models, it has limitations in terms of addressing dataset shift
issues. Furthermore, it does not investigate the architecture’s
practicalityincloudenvironmentsoritscompatibilitywithother
defencemechanisms.
Elmasryetal.[ 15]introducedanadvancedcloud-basedstrategyto
safeguardcloudenvironmentsfromnumerousmaliciousthreats.
It is composed of five main modules: monitoring, processing,
analysis, prediction, and response. For added protection, a
third-party intrusion detection system is employed. The sys-
tem combines signature-based methods and anomaly detection
techniques. Its performance surpasses empirical test results on
NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017 datasets. However, the system has
some limitations such as possible misclassification by outdated
signature-basedrulesandcomputationalresourcerequirements.
Vashishthaetal.[ 16]proposedahybridintrusiondetectionmodel
for cloud systems. The model combines signature-based and
anomaly-based models. It outperformed existing techniques in
the tests that were done using three datasets. Nevertheless, it
has limitations, like difficulty of scalability and adaptability to
differentcloudarchitectures,notrelevanttonewtypesofattacks,
anditlacksrealisticvalidationandanalysisoffalsepositivesand
falsenegatives.
Maheswari et al. [17] proposed an effective IDS to secure web
and cloud computing. The system uses multi-verse and chaotic
atom search optimization for data pre-processing, and uses
chaotic manta-ray foraging optimization for clustering. It also
incorporates modified teacher learning for attack labelling. The
systemoutperformspreviousstudiesusingKDDCup’99andNSL-
KDDdatasets.However,theauthorsdidnotexaminethesystem’s
scalabilityorattackresistance.
Elbakri et al. [18] presented a novel framework utilizing a
supportvectormachinetheprunedexactlineartimealgorithmto
enhancedaccuracyandefficiency.Itisformedoffourkeycompo-
nents:featureselection,surveillance,trainingset,andtestingset.
Ahybridharmonysearchalgorithmandthesymmetricaluncer-
tainty filter are used in the framework to distinguish between
normalandabnormalnetworktrafficinthecloudenvironment.
The proposed scheme identifies change points within network
trafficdata,enablingtheidentificationofanomaliesandpotential
security threats with improved precision. The system has been
assessed using the NSL-KDD and ISOT-CID datasets in terms
of accuracy, precision, and recall where it outperforms existing
detectiontechniques.

∙ Blockchainandsmartcontract
TheappearanceofBlockchaintechnologywasaresponsetothe
centralizedsystemslimitations,ensuringtrust,transparency,and
resilience. It was introduced in 2008 by Satoshi Nakamoto as
thefoundationalinfrastructureforBitcoin,providingadecentral-
ized framework for recording and verifying digital transactions
without any need of intermediaries [57]. The great features of
immutability, transparency, and distributed trust facilitated its
swift integration across various sectors such as finance, energy,
healthcare,smartcityinfrastructures,andcloudenvironments.
A blockchain is fundamentally a distributed wallet composed
of a sequence of blocks, each block contains a list of validated
transactions, a timestamp, a cryptographic hash of the previous
block, a Merkle root for efficient transaction verification, a
nonce (number used once), and other metadata [57, 58]. All
participating devices have a replicated version of this chain;
whichallowseliminatinganysinglepointoffailureandensuring
fault tolerance. Hence, altering historical data would require
modifying all subsequent blocks across the majority of nodes,
which is computationally impossible under standard consensus
mechanisms. The blockchain systems consist of layers. The
data layer organizes and secures transactions using hash chains
andMerkletrees.Thecommunicationsandtransactionsamong
peers are guaranty by the network layer. The consensus layer
ensures agreement on a single valid version of the chain, even
in the presence of malicious or failing nodes. The execution of
SCs, enabling automated and self-enforcing operations, are all
supported by the contract layer. Finally, the application layer
offers end-user interfaces and services such as decentralized
finance,identityverification,anddistributedstorage[ 58].

framework to quantify belief and plausibility based on evidence
subsets, which is more suitable for combining the varied risk
indicators within our system. Preliminary modelling confirmed
DST’s effectiveness in handling the inherent uncertainty and
heterogeneityofriskdatainthiscontext.
In our proposition, we apply the DST to SCs for dynamic
risk assessment and threshold adjustment. These self-executing
contracts use DST concepts to assess and respond to risks in
real-time. By aggregating data from cloud nodes, the contracts
calculatebeliefvaluesusingthebelieffunction,estimatepotential
risksusingtheplausibilityfunction,andusefocalelementsand
themassfunctionforaccurateriskassessment.Thisintegration
enhances the system’s responsiveness and resilience against
cloudattacks,ensuringeffectiveandreliableriskmanagement.

assault scenarios. Security enhancements are constantly visible
since risk-based decision making responds very fast. Dynamic
protectionsincreaseusabilitywithoutcompromisingrobustness.
